How PBMs Extract Money

Spread Pricing

PBMs charge insurers one price for a drug while paying pharmacies less, keeping the "spread" as profit. Ohio found PBMs charged Medicaid $208 million more than they paid pharmacies for the same drugs.

Rebate Manipulation

Drug companies pay rebates to PBMs to get preferred formulary placement. PBMs keep most rebates instead of passing savings to patients, incentivizing higher list prices.

Pharmacy Network Control

PBMs force patients to use their own pharmacies by restricting network access. CVS Caremark directs patients to CVS stores, OptumRx to Optum pharmacies.

Administrative Fees

PBMs charge multiple fees for processing, prior authorization, formulary management, and data services. These fees add billions in costs with no patient benefit.

Vertical Integration

The Big 3 PBMs are owned by health insurers, creating conflicts of interest. Higher drug costs mean higher insurance premiums, maximizing profits across their integrated businesses.
